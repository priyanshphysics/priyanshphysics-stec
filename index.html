Title
STEC: Space-Time Engineered Computer — A Hybrid Numerical Architecture for Coupled Classical, Quantum, Relativistic and Cosmological Research
Abstract
STEC is a research-computing architecture combining a virtual instruction engine, numerical field evolution, quantum-mechanical toy models, scenario analysis, persistent datasets and an observable activity trace. A simulation result alone is not a new law of physics; physical claims require mathematical consistency, numerical validation, comparison with established theory and independent observation or experiment.

Method
Question → Hypothesis → Equations → Initial/Boundary Conditions → Numerical Solver → Convergence → Dataset → Validation → Error Analysis → Prediction → Experiment/Observation
Space-Time: What Does STEC Measure?
STEC does not directly “see” spacetime. A computational model can evolve mathematical quantities such as metric components, curvature-related quantities, geodesic separation, proper-time relationships, gravitational-wave strain, or field propagation. In this browser build, outputs are numerical model quantities rather than measurements of actual spacetime.
Future Research Effect
Every future effect is conditional: given assumptions A+B+C, the model produces D after N simulated steps. Results are labelled as hypotheses, numerical results or experimentally established facts rather than mixing them.
What Could Become a New Result?
A potentially new result would require reproducible deviation from established predictions that survives numerical convergence checks and independent verification. STEC can search for such deviations; it cannot declare a discovery merely because a graph changes.
